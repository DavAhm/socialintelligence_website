Core Research Areas

What We Investigate

Our lab combines behavioral paradigms, computational modeling, virtual reality, EEG, and fMRI to dissect how social dynamics emerge.

Pillar 01

Group Influences on Individual Behavior

How does being in a crowd or group alter individual decision-making? We examine social and motor contagion, conformity behavior, and how fundamental processes like the sense of agency transform in social environments.

Learn More ↗
Pillar 02

Social Interactions & Joint Action

Investigating dyadic coordination, kinematic adaptation, and nonverbal synchrony when two or more agents collaborate. We explore how agent properties, artificial agents, and social exclusion influence shared action spaces.

Learn More ↗
Pillar 03

Intentional Control & Free Will

To what degree can humans make decisions freely and intentionally regulate their thoughts? We explore voluntary action selection, the cognitive neuroscience of volition, and how beliefs in free will shape moral accountability.

Learn More ↗
Methodology

Experimental Paradigms & Tools

We combine behavioral, physiological, neural, and immersive methods to study social behavior.

Motion Capture

Optical motion tracking of the whole body to record the timing and kinematics of movements, for example during joint action, imitation, or dance.

Virtual Reality (VR)

Fully immersive, interactive environments with controlled social avatars, allowing precise manipulation of visual cues, group size, and proxemics.

Electroencephalogram (EEG)

High temporal-resolution recordings of brain activity to capture readiness potentials, event-related dynamics, and learning-related neural signals.

Eye-Tracking

Wearable and screen-based gaze tracking and pupillometry to quantify joint attention, visual search, and arousal during social interaction.

Interoceptive Measures

Heart activity (ECG) and other bodily signals to study how we perceive and voluntarily control the internal states of our body.

Online Experiments

Web-based studies that let us test large and diverse samples on perception, decision-making, agency, and moral judgments.
